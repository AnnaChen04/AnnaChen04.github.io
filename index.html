Research Interests

Modeling Ocean Biogeochemistry, Carbon, and Temperature

My research centers on modeling the ocean carbon and biogeochemical cycles. I am most interested in understanding and improving hindcast and forecast models, particularly disentangling impacts from internal variability from that of model structure; in resolving regional-scale biogeochemical fluxes with tractable statistical and algebraic methods; and in understanding how sparse and uneven observational coverage propagates into our model estimates.

For my PhD work, I want to utilize the full range of available data — Argo floats, satellite retrievals, and ship-track and bottle measurements — and to characterize the distinct uncertainties each source carries. The Arctic and the Southern Ocean draw my curiosity most, since both remain sparsely observed and biogeochemically complex. Having also worked in paleoceanographic reconstruction, I would like to potentially bring state-of-the-art modeling methods to bear on constraining uncertainty in the paleo ocean as well.

1 — Surface ocean pCO2 sets the air–sea carbon flux, and observation-based statistical reconstructions of it are validated against model testbeds (McKinley Lab). 2 — Phytoplankton in the euphotic zone fix carbon; the temperature and pCO2 conditions where they grow determine how much (Hurley Lab). 3 — Temperature proxies preserved in seafloor sediment record how the surface ocean behaved during past warm intervals (McManus Lab).
